The body heaves, pitches and rolls on four independent corners, each with a coil spring and a 2-way damper acting through the motion ratio squared. An anti-roll bar per axle resists only the difference between left and right travel. On the circuit the car also moves in the plane: each tire makes cornering force from its slip angle, capped by its vertical load, and a tire with more load gains grip less than in proportion, which is why load transfer, and so the springs and bars, changes the balance. Each wheel turns at its own speed: brake and drive torque against the road give a slip ratio, and slip ratio and slip angle share one grip budget, so a locked or spinning wheel has almost no cornering grip left. Grip also depends on camber against the road: a cornering tire grips best leaning slightly into the turn, so body roll, the wishbones' camber gain, static camber and caster all matter. The front uprights turn about the axis through their ball joints, driven by a rack and tie rods; toe and bump steer add to each wheel's steering angle. The circuit has gentle hills, rough patches and raised kerbs; the grass sits 3 cm below the track and grips about half as well. A car that leaves the track is put back on the racing line half a second later, and that lap does not count. Not modelled: a limited-slip differential, aero, camber thrust and kingpin inclination. Roll and pitch centres sit at ground level.
